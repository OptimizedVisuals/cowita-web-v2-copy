Why CoWiTa

Talent is everywhere. Opportunity isn't.

A young person can have the instinct to dance, the voice to sing, the imagination to draw, the words to write or the confidence to perform — and still have no clear path for what comes next.

CoWiTa exists to help close that gap. We start with what a young person already has, then build the skills, confidence, guidance and opportunities needed for that talent to grow into something meaningful.

The gap

Being talented is only the beginning.

For many young people, the hardest part is not discovering that they can do something. It is finding someone who takes that ability seriously enough to help them develop it.

A child may spend hours dancing in a living room. A teenager may fill exercise books with drawings. Someone may write poems that nobody outside their phone ever reads. Another young person may be the one everyone asks to perform at school, church or a community event.

These moments are often treated as hobbies — something enjoyable to do after the “real work” is finished. But talent does not automatically become ability, and ability does not automatically become opportunity.

Without guidance, practice, constructive feedback, exposure and a chance to create something real, potential can remain exactly that: potential.

The real challenge

The problem is not always a lack of ability.

Sometimes it is the absence of a pathway between what a young person can already do and what that ability could become.

01

Talent without structure

Natural ability can grow quickly at first, but meaningful development requires deliberate practice, feedback and consistency.

02

Skill without direction

A young person can become good at something without knowing how that ability connects to education, work, creative opportunities or a larger purpose.

03

Potential without exposure

Creative growth becomes difficult to sustain when there are few spaces to practise publicly, collaborate, receive feedback or have work seen.

04

Ability without opportunity

Even a strong skill needs pathways into real experiences, commissions, performances, collaborations and other forms of opportunity.

Why start with talent?

Because potential is personal.

We believe young people are more than a list of needs waiting to be solved.

They also carry imagination, curiosity, ability, personality, lived experience and ideas. When development starts from those strengths, learning can become more personal — and therefore more meaningful.

CoWiTa is built around that principle. We do not begin by asking, “What programme should this young person fit into?”

We begin by asking:

What can they already do? What could they become? What would help them get there?

From talent to possibility

Talent needs somewhere to go.

CoWiTa is designed around the journey that follows discovery.

01

Discover

Recognise the ability that is already there — sometimes before the young person has fully recognised it themselves.

02

Develop

Turn instinct into skill through practice, mentorship, learning, feedback and the discipline required to improve.

03

Create

Give that developing ability a reason to exist beyond practice: a performance, piece of work, story, project or creative output.

04

Be seen

Create pathways for young creatives to share their work, collaborate with others and experience what it means to have an audience.

05

Find opportunity

Connect growing ability with practical possibilities — from collaborations and commissions to performances and future work.

06

Keep growing

The goal is not one successful moment. It is a creative journey that can continue through learning, opportunity, income and reinvestment in the person.

What this means in practice

A dancer is not just someone who dances.

The same principle applies across every creative discipline.

Dance

From movement to expression.

Development can move from natural movement into technique, choreography, performance, collaboration and a stronger understanding of what the dancer can communicate through their body.

Music

From a voice to a craft.

A natural ear or strong voice can become disciplined musical ability through training, rehearsal, performance, collaboration and opportunities to create.

Visual Art

From imagination to work.

Drawing can become a language, a body of work and eventually a practical creative skill when young artists have space to experiment, receive feedback and share what they make.

Writing

From thoughts to stories.

A young writer can develop from simply having something to say into understanding voice, structure, storytelling, editing and the power of written work.

Acting

From confidence to performance.

Acting can develop confidence, communication, discipline, empathy and storytelling while creating a practical foundation for performance and production.

Beyond creativity

Talent also needs life skills.

Creative ability becomes more sustainable when young people also understand communication, collaboration, discipline, personal development, opportunity and how to navigate the world around their craft.

Why mentorship matters

Sometimes potential needs someone to see it before the world does.

A mentor does more than teach a skill.

The right guidance can help a young person understand where they are, recognise where they can improve, ask better questions and see possibilities they may not have considered before.

That is why mentorship is not an optional extra in the CoWiTa model. It is part of creating an environment where talent can develop with direction.

Our difference

We are not trying to create one definition of success.

A young person's creative journey can lead in different directions. Our role is to help make those possibilities more visible and more achievable.

01

Person before programme

We start with the young person and their ability rather than asking them to fit neatly into a predefined category.

02

Development before exposure

Being seen matters, but visibility without growth is fragile. We care about the work behind the moment.

03

Opportunity beyond inspiration

Inspiration can start a journey. Practical opportunities are what help a young person continue it.

04

Growth that can continue

The long-term goal is not dependency on CoWiTa. It is helping young people build the confidence, skills and networks to keep moving forward.

The bigger picture

When a young person develops their talent, the impact can travel further than the individual.

A skill can create confidence. Confidence can create initiative. Initiative can create work. Work can create opportunity for others.

That is why CoWiTa sees talent development as more than an arts activity. Creative development can contribute to personal growth, livelihoods, collaboration, community identity and a stronger culture of young people creating rather than simply consuming.

Kenya's wider creative-economy agenda increasingly recognises youth talent, skills development, entrepreneurship and creative opportunity as important areas of development.
